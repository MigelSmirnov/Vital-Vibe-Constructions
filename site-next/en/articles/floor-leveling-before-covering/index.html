The floor looks finished, until you look at the skirting

A room can look well finished from a distance. Closer to the skirting, a gap appears. Three job examples show why floor preparation deserves its own item in the estimate.

Vital Vibe ConstructionPublished on September 13, 2026
In this article

  2. 02First example: around 14 cm over 10 metres
  3. 03Second example: a thin self-leveling layer
  4. 04Define the base before choosing the finish
  5. 05What the estimate should make clear

01At first glance, everything looks fine

In this room, the floor covering is installed and the skirting appears to complete the finish. The overall view does not clearly show what happens at floor level.

Up close, a gap is visible beneath the skirting. The tool points it out; it does not measure its height.

On this job we worked as the floor-installation contractor. We recommended leveling the base before installation, but the decision about that preparation was not ours. According to the account of this job, the base was not leveled beforehand. This is a separate property from the two preparation examples below.

02First example: around 14 cm over 10 metres

At another property, the level difference was approximately 14 cm over 10 metres, according to the measurement supplied by the person responsible for the work. The photograph documents leveling in progress. The laser line provides a reference, but the measurement cannot be read from the image.

The following photograph shows the same property after the floor covering and skirting were installed. Tools and unfinished work are still visible: this is the floor result, not a completed property handover.

03Second example: a thin self-leveling layer

These two photographs show another property. They document application of a thin self-leveling compound and the freshly spread surface.

We do not assign a measured thickness or mixing ratio to these images. This thin finish and correction of the large level difference in the previous example are different scopes of work; the photos are not interchangeable installation instructions.

04Define the base before choosing the finish

Our estimates separate base preparation from floor installation. Before specifying a solution, we review the substrate condition and differences in level. In an existing building, added weight and material compatibility are part of the project review.

The system and thickness are defined for each property according to product instructions and substrate conditions. These photographs document particular jobs; they do not prescribe a solution for every home.

05What the estimate should make clear

  • Which irregularities were measured and where.
  • What base preparation is included.
  • Which system and thickness are specified for this property.
  • What conditions must be met before installing the covering.
  • How skirting, doors and transitions to other floors will be handled.

A general photograph of the finish does not replace checking the details. That is why we show both the room and the joint between the floor and the skirting.
